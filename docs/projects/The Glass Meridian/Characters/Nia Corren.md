# Character Summary

Nia Corren is an investigative reporter known for long-form civic accountability work. She has been tracking unusual property acquisitions in East Reach without knowing the pattern is connected to Meridian.

## Canonical Identity

| Field | Canon |
| --- | --- |

| Age at story opening | 39 |
| Occupation | Investigative reporter |
| Narrative role | External investigator and pressure source |

## Core Character Concept

A reporter comfortable publishing uncertainty collides with a cartographer who wants proof before accusation.

## Family & Background

Nia grew up outside Lumen and came to the city for university. She stayed because the aftermath of Nightwater reshaped the city faster than its institutions could document.

## Education & Career

Studied journalism and public policy. Built her reputation through records-driven investigations of municipal contracting.

## Important Life Events

- Covered the tenth anniversary of Nightwater.
- Exposed procurement irregularities in the Harbor Authority.
- Began tracking East Reach property acquisitions six months before the novel opens.

## Key Relationships

**Mara Vale:** Former university acquaintance and uneasy investigative partner.

**Commissioner Orin Saye:** Frequent source of official denials.

## Professional & Practical Strengths

Public records, source cultivation, financial records, interviews, and cross-institution pattern recognition.

## Personality

### Strengths

Persistent, socially agile, skeptical, brave.

### Flaws

Can push sources too hard and sometimes treats timing as part of the reporting strategy rather than a moral problem.

### Contradictions

She values verification but knows waiting for perfect certainty can protect powerful people.

## Psychological & Behavioral Habits

Records voice notes while walking and writes names in the margins of printed records.

## Life Outside the Main Story

Keeps irregular hours and knows nearly every late-night café near the civic district.

## Character Arc & Progression

### Stage One

Treats Meridian as another contracting story.

### Stage Two

Recognizes the human stakes behind the data.

### Stage Three

Learns to coordinate publication timing with emergency consequences.

### Stage Four

Helps release evidence without collapsing the evacuation message.

### Stage Five

Continues investigating the post-storm accountability process.

## Voice & Dialogue Notes

Fast, conversational, and comfortable asking blunt questions. Often frames evidence as competing explanations.

## Continuity Notes

Nia does not know about the glass compass until Mara shows it to her.
