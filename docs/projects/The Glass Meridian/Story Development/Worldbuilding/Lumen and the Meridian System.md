# Worldbuilding Summary

Lumen is a coastal city that has adapted to increasingly severe storm surge through barriers, elevated transit, distributed sensors, and aggressive data-driven emergency planning.

## Canonical Principles

- Meridian is useful and generally accurate.
- Meridian does not invent floods or fabricate sensor readings.
- The manipulation occurs in how uncertainty is translated into public risk categories and investment priorities.
- Raw data, model outputs, policy thresholds, and public-facing maps are distinct layers.
- No single individual controls the entire system.
- The old brass survey network predates Meridian and provides an independent physical reference.

## Setting

Lumen is a near-future coastal city of old masonry districts, high-tech civic infrastructure, active port traffic, and increasingly severe storms.

## History

The Nightwater Flood transformed public policy twelve years before the main story.

## Geography

A central ridge divides higher civic districts from low eastern floodplain neighborhoods. The harbor opens toward a funnel-shaped estuary that can amplify storm surge.

## Cultures & Societies

Residents are accustomed to evacuation drills, flood gates, insurance maps, and storm alerts. Different districts have very different levels of trust in municipal resilience policy.

## Governments & Institutions

The Lumen Civic Government owns Meridian's public mission. Helix Municipal Systems maintains key proprietary components under contract.

## Economy & Technology

Shipping, marine repair, civic technology, insurance analytics, tourism, and redevelopment dominate the city's economy.

## Belief Systems

Public faith in expertise grew after Nightwater, but East Reach residents increasingly see resilience policy as a language used to justify displacement.

## Rules & Systems

Meridian ingests tidal gauges, rainfall, offshore buoys, drainage flow, barrier status, building and population data, and evacuation capacity. A proprietary optimization layer converts probabilistic forecasts into simplified public zones.

## Conflict & Pressure Points

Simplified maps conceal uncertainty. Property markets react strongly to risk-zone changes. Officials fear public confusion during emergencies. East Reach residents suspect risk designations are accelerating managed retreat.

## Story-Relevant Canon

Bellwether Row disappears because a redevelopment layer marks it as transition inventory, causing the public renderer to omit active residential parcels under a specific risk-state configuration.

## Continuity Notes

The omission is a plausible interaction between classification and rendering, not fabricated telemetry or supernatural behavior.
